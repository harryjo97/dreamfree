What makes imagination-free WAMs scale?

Skipping video generation enables fast inference. It also avoids reliance on potentially inaccurate video predictions.
Making stronger video priors useful for control is the next challenge.

Making video priors useful without generating future videos.

In imagination-free WAMs, actions are predicted without generating future videos. Denoising videos well during co-training does not guarantee that the shared features used for action prediction capture future information.

DreamFree uses future representation distillation to explicitly guide these features to capture the video diffusion model’s predictive priors. In our RoboCasa evaluation, scaling the backbone from 5B to 14B raises DreamFree’s success from 58.5% to 71.7%, while Fast-WAM changes only from 44.4% to 44.7%.

DreamFree achieves these gains without embodied pre-training.

14B video priors at 11 Hz.

DreamFree achieves 11 Hz on a single H200 and a 17.8× inference speedup over DreamZero, while improving real-world success. Imagination-free inference enables nearly continuous control without video step distillation or asynchronous execution.

A 14B backbone on 48 GB.

Omitting future-video generation and classifier-free guidance (CFG) reduces the inference memory footprint. The policy can fit on a single A6000 with 48 GB of memory; all reported experiments use H200 GPUs.

Read the full abstract +

World Action Models (WAM) use the predictive knowledge of pretrained video diffusion models for robot control through future video prediction. However, iterative video denoising is costly, and inaccurate visual predictions can compromise action prediction. Our key idea is to make this predictive knowledge available through visual representations computed from the current observation, enabling action prediction without generating future videos.

We introduce DreamFree, a WAM that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a predictive visual encoder through representation distillation. The visual encoder learns to predict a frozen video diffusion model’s representation using only the current observation. An action module is then trained to predict actions by extracting action-relevant information from the visual encoder. Across real-world and simulated tasks, DreamFree achieves higher success rates than state-of-the-art VLAs and WAMs, while performing real-time closed-loop control at 11Hz with a 14B video diffusion backbone. Experiments show that DreamFree can translate stronger video priors and additional video data into better robot control and enable learning new robot tasks from video demonstrations, including videos from a different embodiment, without action annotation for the new tasks.

Make video priors useful
without imagining videos.

An explicit alignment loss makes current-frame features predictive of the future.

STAGE 1

Distill predictive representations

A frozen video diffusion teacher observes current and future frames. An alignment loss trains the current-frame features to predict the teacher’s future-aware representations, alongside the original video generative objective. This directly supervises the features used for control and needs no action labels.

STAGE 2

Learn to read out actions

Using action-labeled robot demonstrations, a readout module extracts action-relevant features from the visual encoder. An action decoder maps these features to executable action chunks.

AT INFERENCE

Current observation → Predictive visual encoder → Action module → Robot action

Predictive video knowledge in a single visual pass, followed by action decoding.

Across 24 kitchen tasks, DreamFree reaches 71.7% success, rising to 74.4% with extra video data. Scaling the backbone from 5B to 14B adds 13.2 percentage points, compared with 0.3 for Fast-WAM.

Can DreamFree also benefit from video-only data?

Like other WAMs, DreamFree can benefit from video-only demonstrations through its predictive encoder.
These results suggest that learning demonstrated task plans may help compose familiar skills into new tasks without additional action labels.

Atomic tasks teach individual skills. Composite tasks combine them in a sequence.

Composite videos in pre-training video-only fine-tuning

We add composite-task demonstrations to visual encoder pre-training, alongside atomic-task videos. Both models use the same atomic-task action labels to train the action module; no composite-task action labels are used.
